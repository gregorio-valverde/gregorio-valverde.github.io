Some time ago, I had the opportunity to participate as a speaker at the first Grupo Hozono Global Professional Congress, where I shared part of the work we were developing around digital transformation and data analytics.

Over time, one of the main reflections I took from that experience was the importance of **communication within technical roles**.

In our daily work, we constantly face complex problems, often in uncertain environments. We work with data, processes, systems, integrations, exceptions and business rules that we end up understanding in considerable depth.

That complexity, together with our own technical knowledge, leads us to build a particular way of understanding the problem. A way that is not always easy to communicate to someone outside the development process.

This is where I believe a fundamental part of our work appears: **being able to transform technical complexity into a simple and useful message**.

During the Congress, for example, we showed how we were using Power BI and data analytics to better understand absenteeism costs, identify patterns and provide more information for decision-making.

Behind all of this there is technical work: data extraction and transformation, modelling, KPI definition, automation and information validation. But that was not really the most important message.

What mattered was explaining **what all that work actually made possible**.

A pipeline does not create value simply because it runs correctly. A dashboard does not create value just because it contains good indicators. Technology starts to create value when it helps **save time, reduce errors, better understand a process or improve decision-making**.

That is why I believe technical professionals cannot limit themselves only to code.

Process optimisation is not a simple path. It requires understanding both technology and the needs of the people who use our solutions. It also requires a degree of empathy: understanding who is in front of us and adapting the way we communicate what we have built.

In some situations, we will need to talk about architecture, data models or performance. In others, we will need to talk about costs, time, risks or decision-making capacity.

In the end, the technical solution may be the same, but the way we communicate it should not be.

I am increasingly convinced that building a good solution is only one part of the job. We also need to be able to communicate **why it matters and what real impact it has on the company**.

Because the final goal is not simply to work with data.

It is to make data help improve the business.
