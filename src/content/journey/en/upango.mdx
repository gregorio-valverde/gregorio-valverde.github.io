I begin my first professional experience with a one-month internship and an apparently simple task: designing an internal database for reporting.

This is where I really enter the world of Data Warehousing. Using Microsoft Fabric, I design fact and dimension tables from ERP and employee portal data. I also use Spark to clean and transform data obtained from the ERP API, completing the project with performance and cost reports.

After being hired, I move directly into consulting work, developing and improving ETLs and new BI solutions for clients.

During this stage I help introduce new tools and ways of working, particularly Apache Airflow for orchestrating integrations and data processes.

This is probably the point when I confirm that I genuinely enjoy this field. Not only because of the technical work, but also because I enjoy understanding business problems, speaking with clients and turning fairly ambiguous needs into concrete solutions.
